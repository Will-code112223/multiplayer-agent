# Build AskOnce on the Room You Already Have

**Bottom line:** build **AskOnce**, a shared room where one agent answers the "how does this work here?" questions that teammates would otherwise interrupt a colleague for, and when it genuinely has to ask a human, it captures that answer back into the team's docs with a record of who said it and who approved it, so the question is never asked twice. It should be built as a narrow extension of multiplayergpt rather than from scratch, because the repository already contains the exact architecture that 2026 hackathon judges rewarded (a human approval gate on agent actions with an audit record, live streamed reasoning, a write-back target, and a test suite), which lets three builders spend the 12 hours on one memorable demo moment instead of on plumbing. The bottleneck is well sourced: **53–56% of knowledge workers say the only way to get information is to ask someone or schedule a meeting, and roughly a quarter of the workweek goes to searching for it** ([Atlassian State of Teams 2025 via Unleash](https://www.unleash.ai/artificial-intelligence/atlassian-ai-could-help-workers-stop-wasting-2-4-billion-hours-searching-for-information-every-year/)), while the average worker is interrupted about **every two minutes** ([Microsoft WorkLab via CNN](https://edition.cnn.com/2025/06/17/business/microsoft-report-infinite-workday-intl)). The live demo moment is judges scanning a QR code onto their own phones, joining the room, asking a question the docs cannot answer, watching the agent route it to one named human instead of broadcasting, and then watching the answer land in a doc with an approval stamp before a second judge asks the same thing and gets it in two seconds. The more spectacular alternative, a voice agent that waits on hold for you, has the best "before" numbers of any idea in the notes but is the wrong pick for today specifically: the IRS, SSA, DMV and most clinics are closed on a Sunday evening, so the core step of that demo cannot be real on stage, and judges penalize mocks they can detect. The grand prize is an investor interview, and speedrun's own partners say they screen for velocity, a felt problem, and a narrow wedge that a small team can own; AskOnce is pitched to a panel of founders who are each, personally, the human API for their own company.

## Recent winners close a loop on real work and show the gate

Across roughly thirty well-sourced winners from early 2025 to September 2026, the winning shape shifted from spectacle to supervised work. The 2026 corporate hackathons converged on one archetype: an agent inside the system of record that diagnoses, fixes, and writes the result back, with visible rigor. GitLab's grand prize **LORE** routed questions through eight agents with loop prevention, a dashboard and "a CLI shipping with 43 tests," and its sibling winner Gitdefender "spots the bug, writes the fix, and opens the code review" inside the merge request ([GitLab](https://about.gitlab.com/blog/gitlab-ai-hackathon-2026-meet-the-winners/)). DataHub's grand prize **Project Blackbox** localizes a data incident, "proves root cause against a machine-enforced evidence gate," repairs the code, opens a pull request, and writes the incident back to the catalog ([DataHub](https://datahub.com/blog/meet-the-winners-of-build-with-datahub-the-agent-hackathon/)). Microsoft's grand prize **PRism** is a multi-agent release gate that "looks at a pull request and asks what could break in the real world" ([Microsoft](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/azure-events/announcing-the-ai-dev-days-hackathon-winners/4513528)). Anthropic summarized its Opus 4.7 winners as combining "multi-agent orchestration, persistent memory, MCP tools, sandboxed execution, and smart prompt[ing]" ([ClaudeDevs on X](https://x.com/ClaudeDevs/status/2049644696260235413)). The notes' inference is the right one: judges in 2026 reward agent architecture hygiene (a gate, a sandbox, a write-back, tests) more than model choice, and a 12-hour build can borrow that credibility cheaply with one gate, one write-back and a passing test suite rather than eight agents.

Short in-person events are a different sport, and SpeedHacks is a 12-hour one. There, the project whose demo is the product wins: **FaceTimeOS** took Cal Hacks 12.0 with a live FaceTime call into a Mac that acts on voice commands ([Dylan Lu](https://blog.dylanlu.com/cal-hacks-12/)); **GibberLink** won the ElevenLabs × a16z hackathon with two agents switching to data-over-sound mid-call, and the judges were "unanimous" among 300+ projects ([ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/announcing-the-winners-of-the-elevenlabs-worldwide-hackathon)); **Browser Brawl** put two browser agents in a live arena at the Browser Use × YC overnight event ([MiniMax](https://www.minimax.io/news/minimax-ycombinator-hackathon-building-the-future-of-web)); **Good Omen** built a journalist tool in 20 hours that a judge-side company acquired on the spot for €20k ([Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/lovable-project-europe-hackathon-winners)); a no-code game designer won a 3-hour Stanford × DeepMind sprint by shipping a working app inside the window ([Medium, host account](https://medium.com/@elviraux/the-people-winning-ai-hackathons-in-2026-arent-the-strongest-engineers-anymore-3fc43f1d13d7)). The only a16z-affiliated hackathon precedent is the 2024 ElevenLabs consumer-AI event, whose podium included **Two Somethin'**, an AI handling customer-service calls, next to a fitness game and voice Pokémon ([ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/hackathons-nyc-london)). The recurring live-demo beat in productivity winners is "watch the artifact appear where I already work," and the second beat is visible reasoning plus a gate that refuses to act without proof.

What judges say separated winners is remarkably consistent and mostly about the pitch minutes, not the build hours. The JetBrains × Codex judging post holds that "a strong project with a confusing demo loses to a simpler project that the judges understand," that judges should be made to share the frustration with the problem, that something should work within about 90 seconds, and that the right starting question is "what's something I can build now that I probably couldn't have built before?" ([JetBrains](https://blog.jetbrains.com/ai/2026/06/how-to-win-a-hackathon-notes-from-the-judging-table/)). A judge who scored three 2026 hackathons found a working live demo "the biggest differentiator," that a one-line identity is what survives the "which one was the scheduling one" conversation, and that "a team that built a platform in 36 hours reads as a team that did not make good decisions" ([dev.to, kurbaitaev](https://dev.to/kurbaitaev/what-judges-actually-score-notes-from-a-year-of-hackathon-judging-3p4l)). Another judge's weighted rubric puts a working demo at 30%, problem clarity at 25%, and scope judgment at 20% ([dev.to, pranjulrathour](https://dev.to/pranjulrathour/what-hackathon-judges-actually-look-for-a-rubric-from-both-sides-of-the-table-39o)). Browser Use's Reagan Hsu, a serial winner, says to build from personal pain because "you automatically have a valid pain point, an idea of how to solve it, and a customer: yourself," and to talk to sponsors about which projects bore them ([Browser Use](https://browser-use.com/posts/how-to-win-hackathons)). The saturation signal is equally clear: no 2025–2026 grand-prize winner in the catalog is a meeting notetaker, email-triage bot, or standalone RAG chatbot, and idea-list authors report judges are tired of "AI chatbot for X" ([Reskilll](https://reskilll.com/blogs/hackathon-ideas-that-won-2026-judges-actually-pick-examples/)). The closest thing to knowledge Q&A that won, LORE, won on routing, loop prevention, dashboard and tests, not on being a chatbot. That is the bar AskOnce must clear, and the capture-and-write-back loop is how it clears it.

## The best-sourced bottlenecks, and why the service-line ideas fail on a Sunday

The 2025–2026 evidence points at four dominant time sinks that are not where most AI products sit. Interruptions: Microsoft's 2025 WorkLab data put the average worker at an interruption roughly **every two minutes, about 275 a day, 117 emails a day, with ad hoc calls making up around 60% of meetings** ([CNN](https://edition.cnn.com/2025/06/17/business/microsoft-report-infinite-workday-intl); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/microsoft-study-finds-whats-stopping-us-from-being-productive-at-work-is-work)). Searching: Atlassian's 2025 State of Teams found executives and teams spend about a quarter of the workweek searching for information, **2.4 billion hours a year across the Fortune 500**, with about half of workers saying teams have unknowingly duplicated work and 53–56% saying the only way to get information is to ask someone or schedule a meeting ([Unleash](https://www.unleash.ai/artificial-intelligence/atlassian-ai-could-help-workers-stop-wasting-2-4-billion-hours-searching-for-information-every-year/)). App switching: **68% of knowledge workers spend at least 30 minutes a day toggling between tools** ([Slack](https://slack.com/intl/zh-sg/blog/productivity/powering-productivity-workplace)). Onboarding: technical hires take **4.8 months to reach normal productivity** ([Linux Foundation via TechClass](https://www.techclass.com/resources/learning-and-development-articles/onboarding-for-technical-teams-reducing-time-to-full-productivity)). Against that, the saving workers actually report from AI is modest, with most users in the Census Bureau's March 2026 survey saving one to two hours a week ([Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2026/08/ai-use-at-work.html)), which is precisely the opening for a demo that collapses one named task from a two-hour wait to forty seconds. Two more numbers explain why a supervised agent room is the right framing for founder-judges: Asana's 2025 polling found roughly two-thirds of workers believe AI agents are unreliable while they expect to delegate 32% of tasks within a year ([Futurum](https://futurumgroup.com/insights/will-asanas-teammates-drive-better-collaboration-between-humans-and-ai-agents/)), and Gartner expects **more than 40% of agentic AI projects to be cancelled by 2027** for cost, unclear value and weak risk controls ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/ai-story/gartner-agentic-ai-projects-cancelled-2027)). A visible approval record is the answer to both.

The "access services" pillar has the most dramatic numbers and the most vivid demo, and it is worth being honest about why it still loses today. IRS hold times rose **81–161% in the 2026 filing season with about 40% of test calls disconnected** ([TaxProf Blog summarizing Mother Jones](https://taxprofblog.aals.org/2026/07/29/mother-jones-the-irs-is-imploding/)); a Senate staff test found SSA callers waited an average of **102 minutes** for an answered call ([Warren.senate.gov](https://www.warren.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/social_security_war_room_-_wait_times_report.pdf)); new-patient physician appointments average **31 days** ([AMN Healthcare](https://www.amnhealthcare.com/amn-insights/physician/blog/the-growing-challenges-with-physician-appointment-wait-times/)); a benefits application takes **30 to 110 minutes** depending on the state ([Code for America](https://codeforamerica.org/50states)); physician practices spend **13 hours a week on prior authorization** ([AMA](https://www.ama-assn.org/press-center/ama-press-releases/ama-survey-prior-authorization-reform-pledge-falls-short-physicians)). The capabilities are also new: speech-to-speech models with tool calls shipped in 2026, and Vapi, Retell and Bland all do outbound PSTN calls at $0.05–0.20 per minute ([aiagentrank](https://aiagentrank.io/blog/vapi-vs-retell-vs-bland-2026)), with no venture leader found in consumer "hold for me" ([notes, KQ5](https://www.unleash.ai/artificial-intelligence/atlassian-ai-could-help-workers-stop-wasting-2-4-billion-hours-searching-for-information-every-year/)). But the demo must happen between roughly 7:30pm and 9pm on Sunday, October 11, when the IRS, SSA, DMV and nearly every clinic are closed. The core "this is now possible" step would therefore have to be a team-built fake IVR, and the playbook's resolution of the mocking tension is explicit: mock only slow non-core calls and keep the core step real, because "judges can usually tell what's mocked and they penalise it" ([Ainna](https://ainna.ai/resources/faq/winning-hackathon-guide)). Real-call tail latency is **1.4–2.3 seconds across ElevenLabs, Vapi and Retell** in an independent 2026 benchmark ([OpenBenchmarks](https://openbenchmarks.com/voice-agent-latency/voice-agent-latency-comparison)), a Twilio trial account can only call up to five verified numbers ([Twilio](https://static0.twilio.com/docs/usage/trials.md)), and the voice pipeline would be built from zero in a loud room. That is three uncontrollable risks stacked on the one moment the pitch depends on. The same calendar logic weakens the benefits and prior-auth ideas: filling a real state portal or a real payer form with fake data on stage is not something the team can do, so those demos also fall back to mock surfaces, and none of the three builders has the domain depth that made the Opus 4.6 winners credible, where a lawyer, a cardiologist and a road technician won on knowing what the system should do ([Anthropic](https://claude.com/blog/meet-the-winners-of-our-built-with-opus-4-6-claude-code-hackathon)).

The "work" pillar bottleneck is the one the team, the judges, and the demo audience all feel personally, which is the first criterion every judge source names. Founders of five-person companies are their company's documentation; every Nimble ops hire and every Motherboard field technician asks "how does this work here" and waits on a human. The knowledge-search category is crowded at enterprise scale by Glean, Rovo, Copilot and Notion AI, and the notes flag that risk directly. The under-served seam is not retrieval but capture: the moment a human has to be interrupted anyway, the answer is lost to a DM. A product that treats the interruption as the write event, with a named human answering once and an approval stamp on the doc edit, is a different wedge from indexing what already exists, and it maps onto the Codos "organizational context and memory layer" and Phyvant's enterprise memory closely enough to be a natural Best Use candidate if either sponsors.

## multiplayergpt already contains the architecture that 2026 winners were rewarded for

The repository is a 4,159-line TypeScript app (Node 22, React 19, Vite, `ws`, Anthropic SDK) whose README describes exactly the supervised-agent mechanics the winning catalog rewards: everyone watches the reply stream, messages queue mid-turn, anyone can Stop or Steer, and when the assistant wants to write a file "it pauses and asks the room. Anyone can approve or deny, and the chat shows who decided" (`/home/user/multiplayer-agent/README.md`). The protocol encodes this as a `RunStatus` of `"awaiting-approval"`, a `ToolEntry` with a `"pending-approval"` status and a `decidedBy?: Participant` field, and browser intents `say`, `steer`, `interrupt` and `decide` (`/home/user/multiplayer-agent/shared/protocol.ts`). Tools are registered as a `ToolSpec` with a `requiresApproval` boolean and a `run()` function; `write_file` already requires approval and `list_files` and `read_file` do not (`/home/user/multiplayer-agent/server/tools.ts`), and the turn loop already pauses the run when `spec.requiresApproval` is true (`/home/user/multiplayer-agent/server/turn.ts`). Every change is a sequence-numbered event so a late joiner gets a snapshot and a reconnecting tab asks for "everything after event 412"; sessions persist to disk; a password gate exists via `ACCESS_CODE`; a cloudflared tunnel is documented for sharing a public link; and `npm test` runs four suites covering session replay, features, auth and persistence (`/home/user/multiplayer-agent/package.json`). There is also a scripted demo assistant that runs without an API key and deliberately "exercises the approval path" (`/home/user/multiplayer-agent/server/agent.ts`), which is a free, rehearsable fallback for a dead API that the playbook says to prepare in advance ([dev.to, md8_habibullah](https://dev.to/md8_habibullah/we-had-6-hours-a-dying-api-and-im-ran-rm-rf-we-still-won-gdf)).

Map that against what judges rewarded and the overlap is striking. The "evidence gate" and "release gate" of Blackbox and PRism are, in a 12-hour form, the approval pause with a recorded decider. The "write-back to the catalog" of Blackbox and Hindsight is `write_file` into a shared workspace that everyone can read and download. LORE's "43 tests" credibility is four passing test files. The live-demo beats of FaceTimeOS and Browser Brawl, where judges watch a real surface act, become judges on their own phones in the same room watching the same stream, with late-join history guaranteeing a judge who scans the QR code forty seconds late still sees everything. No from-scratch team can build multi-client state sync, reconnect, approval audit and persistence in 12 hours and still have a demo; the playbook's own starter-kit source estimates teams starting from scratch lose six-plus hours to setup ([Reskilll](https://blogs.reskilll.com/?p=398)), and an 8x winner's plan allocates only the first two hours to freezing scope before building the one "wow" feature ([Medium, codetodeploy](https://medium.com/codetodeploy/the-hackathon-insider-playbook-from-a-8x-winner-de83d138bab5)). Starting from multiplayergpt moves the "one input to one output by 25% of the clock" milestone ([Masai School](https://www.masaischool.com/blog/how-to-win-an-ai-hackathon-in-2026-full-guide/)) to roughly hour 1.5 and frees the afternoon for rehearsal, which several winners credit as decisive.

What is missing is small and specific. There is no `ask_human` tool (a tool that pauses the run until a named participant types an answer, rather than approve/deny), no search tool over the workspace beyond listing and reading files, no provenance footer on doc edits, no ingestion of a team's docs or repo into the session workspace, no QR join affordance, and no counter that makes the time saving visible. Each is a bounded addition to a file that already exists, and the biggest one, `ask_human`, is a new `ToolStatus` value plus one new browser intent, following the pattern `decide` already uses. Two caveats temper the enthusiasm. First, search summaries describe SpeedHacks as "a 12-hour build-from-zero event" ([Tech Week listing](https://www.tech-week.com/calendar/sf/events/speedhacks-the-official-speedrun-hackathon-30c239a4-638a-4bd6-8d51-e15259668f51)) and the notes could not confirm rules on prior code; the team must ask at check-in and disclose multiplayergpt as its own MIT-licensed prior work on the first slide regardless. Speedrun's own screen rewards "evidence that you know how to ship" ([speedrun Substack](https://speedrun.substack.com/p/sr007-apps-wrapped)), so an honest "we built the room last month, we built AskOnce today" is a velocity story, not a liability, provided the rules permit it. Second, judges will ask "isn't this Codos or Glean?" and the one-liner must pre-empt that by putting capture, not search, at its center.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the question from "which bottleneck has the biggest number" to "which bottleneck can be collapsed, for real, in front of founder-judges at 8pm on a Sunday, by three people who already own a supervised-agent room." Under that framing the spectacular voice and government-services ideas lose on the day-of-week and the mock penalty even though they would be the stronger company, and the quieter work-pillar idea wins because its core moment (a human answers once, the doc updates with an approval record, the next asker gets it instantly) is fully real on stage and uses the judges themselves as participants. The deeper implication for the team is that multiplayergpt's real asset is not chat but the audit trail of who steered, stopped, approved and answered; every "agent in the enterprise" company on the panel needs that trail, which is why AskOnce should be pitched as the first wedge of a supervised-agent product rather than as a knowledge tool.

The second implication is that the 12-hour clock should be spent in an unusual ratio: under an hour on scope, about four on the differentiating logic, and the entire last three and a half on seed data, failover, recording and five to ten timed rehearsals, because every judge source agrees the pitch minutes decide the result and because the team's foundation makes that ratio affordable. If the sponsor list at 9am includes a memory or context API, the knowledge-source adapter should be swapped onto it by noon; if it does not, the team should not chase a Best Use prize with a shallow integration, since founder-judges notice the difference.

## Recommendation

### Primary pick: AskOnce

**Name and identity.** AskOnce: a shared room where your team's agent answers the "how does this work here?" questions people would otherwise interrupt a colleague for, and when it has to ask a human, it writes that answer into the docs with a record of who answered and who approved, so nobody is asked twice.

**The bottleneck and its sourced "before."** The target person is the engineer in week two and the senior engineer who is the team's human API. Today the junior either searches (about **a quarter of the workweek** goes to searching for information) or interrupts, and **53–56% of knowledge workers say the only way to get information is to ask someone or schedule a meeting** ([Atlassian State of Teams 2025 via Unleash](https://www.unleash.ai/artificial-intelligence/atlassian-ai-could-help-workers-stop-wasting-2-4-billion-hours-searching-for-information-every-year/)); the senior is already interrupted **about every two minutes** ([Microsoft via CNN](https://edition.cnn.com/2025/06/17/business/microsoft-report-infinite-workday-intl)); and the hire takes **4.8 months to reach normal productivity** ([Linux Foundation via TechClass](https://www.techclass.com/resources/learning-and-development-articles/onboarding-for-technical-teams-reducing-time-to-full-productivity)). The "before" the pitch uses on the slide is the first two numbers; the "after" is measured live during the demo. Because none of these sources time a single question, Person C should also collect a venue tally during the day (see the plan) rather than invent a per-question figure; the notes explicitly advise an in-demo timing over an unsourced national stat.

**The exact live demo moment (target 90 seconds, judge-driven).** Slide shows a QR code; judges scan it and land in the room on their phones, each with a name and color, watching the same stream. The seeded workspace is a small, realistic repo plus docs for a fictional startup with one deliberate gap: the docs explain how to deploy but not where the production feature flags live. The presenter invites a judge to ask anything about "how we deploy"; the agent answers from the docs in seconds with file citations, and the on-screen counter ticks "answered without interrupting anyone." The presenter then asks the gap question. The agent searches, finds nothing, and instead of guessing calls `ask_human`, which pauses the run and posts a one-line question addressed to the one person tagged as the owner of that area, Person B, who answers in one line from a second laptop. The agent proposes a doc edit; the `write_file` approval card appears to the whole room; a judge taps Approve on their phone; the file lands in the Files panel with a footer reading "answered by [B], approved by [judge], 2026-10-11 20:04." A second judge, who joined late and still sees full history, asks the same question and gets the answer in two seconds with the provenance line. The presenter says the sentence that is the product: "That question just got asked for the last time." Everything in that sequence is real; nothing is mocked, and the team says so.

**Why it fits speedrun's judges and the Best Use prizes.** Founders feel this bottleneck personally and so do the operators at Nimble (ops teams in freight forwarding) and Motherboard Labs (field techs organizing SOPs and site history); the Codos "Company Brain" is "an organizational context and memory layer" ([everydev.ai](https://www.everydev.ai/developers/codos-inc)), so AskOnce reads as a wedge into the exact category a judge's company occupies, pitched as capture-at-interruption rather than indexing. The architecture shows what Rauch said defensibility now requires, "the ability to steer models and build verification loops" ([speedrun Substack](https://speedrun.substack.com/p/guillermo-rauchs-5-lessons-for-founders)), and it answers the Gartner "weak risk controls" objection with a visible audit trail. For Best Use, keep the knowledge source behind a single `KnowledgeSource` interface with `search()` and `upsert()`; if Codos, Phyvant or any sponsor exposes a memory or context API at the briefing, swap the adapter onto it by noon and name it on the slide; if no sponsor fits, skip the Best Use chase rather than bolt on a shallow integration.

**Reused from multiplayergpt versus new.** Reused unchanged: the room, link join, presence, streaming, mid-turn queueing, Stop and Steer, the `write_file` approval gate with `decidedBy`, the Files panel, late-join snapshot and reconnect, disk persistence, `ACCESS_CODE`, the scripted fallback assistant, the four test suites, and the documented cloudflared share path (`/home/user/multiplayer-agent/README.md`, `/home/user/multiplayer-agent/server/session.ts`, `/home/user/multiplayer-agent/server/turn.ts`, `/home/user/multiplayer-agent/web/useSession.ts`). New (in order of priority): (1) `ask_human` tool in `server/tools.ts` with a new `ToolStatus` value `"awaiting-answer"`, an `answeredBy` field next to `decidedBy` in `shared/protocol.ts`, an `answer` intent alongside `decide`, and an answer card in `web/Transcript.tsx`; (2) `search_workspace` tool (ripgrep over the session workspace, results with path and line) so the agent cites files rather than reading everything; (3) a provenance footer appended by the server to any `write_file` that follows an `ask_human`; (4) a `seed` script that copies the demo repo and docs into `workspace/<session-id>/` at session creation, plus an "owners" map in Session instructions so the agent knows whom to ask; (5) QR code for the join link in the room header; (6) a small ledger panel counting answered-from-docs, humans-asked, and answers-captured; (7) one new test in `test/features.mjs` covering the ask-answer-write cycle, so the pitch can say the loop is tested. Nothing else: no Slack, no GitHub OAuth, no embeddings, no second model provider UI.

**Tech stack and APIs.** Node 22, TypeScript, React 19, Vite, `ws`, and the Anthropic SDK already wired in `server/agent.ts`; keep the session's web search off during the demo to remove latency and cost. ripgrep for `search_workspace`; a QR library in the client; cloudflared tunnel for the public join link with a phone hotspot as the network, and the LAN-IP URL from `npm start` on port 8787 as the no-internet fallback. Run the server on the presenting laptop, not a cloud host. Set a hard spend cap on the API key before 9am ([dev.to, michaelochieng0](https://dev.to/michaelochieng0/what-building-digicow-at-the-kenya-ai-challenge-taught-me-beyond-writing-code-3hom)).

### Two alternates in brief

**Alternate 1, Hold the Line (access services).** A phone agent that calls a government or clinic line for you, navigates the IVR, waits, and conferences you in, with the call transcript streaming into the multiplayergpt room so family or colleagues can steer it and approve anything sensitive before it is said. It has the strongest "before" numbers in the notes (IRS hold times up 81–161% and ~40% of test calls disconnected in 2026 ([TaxProf Blog](https://taxprofblog.aals.org/2026/07/29/mother-jones-the-irs-is-imploding/)); SSA answered-call waits averaging 102 minutes ([Warren.senate.gov](https://www.warren.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/social_security_war_room_-_wait_times_report.pdf))), a newly possible stack (Vapi, Retell or Bland for outbound PSTN with a custom-LLM webhook into the room server), and no venture leader found. Pick it only if the team can name a real 24/7 line it is allowed to call live and has tested latency and Twilio verification on the venue network before 11am; otherwise the core moment is a mock on a Sunday and the 1.4–2.3 second tail latency ([OpenBenchmarks](https://openbenchmarks.com/voice-agent-latency/voice-agent-latency-comparison)) compounds the risk.

**Alternate 2, Deadline Desk (learn).** A course room where a cohort drops its syllabi and LMS exports and one agent produces a single verified calendar with conflicts flagged, which classmates correct in-room with the corrections written back. Only **9% of students look at the syllabus for deadlines and most consult four different LMS locations** ([Montclair research record](https://researchwith.montclair.edu/en/publications/a-rising-star-how-the-lms-replaced-the-syllabus/)); no agent product was found in the space. It is the cheapest build, reuses the same approval and Files mechanics, needs no external API, and fits the Alpha co-host's student audience, but it will read as small to investors and should be the fallback if prior code is barred or the primary's knowledge seeding proves slow.

### Three-minute pitch outline

The structure combines the judge-notes ordering (one-liner, felt problem, demo inside 90 seconds) with speedrun's seed-pitch elements (why now, wedge, evidence of demand, velocity, ask) ([JetBrains](https://blog.jetbrains.com/ai/2026/06/how-to-win-a-hackathon-notes-from-the-judging-table/); [speedrun Substack](https://speedrun.substack.com/p/how-to-pitch-in-under-2-min); [Paul Graham](https://paulgraham.com/investors.html)).

**0:00–0:10, the one sentence.** "AskOnce is a room where your team's agent answers the questions people would otherwise interrupt you for, and when it has to ask you, it makes sure you're never asked again."

**0:10–0:35, the felt problem.** "Every startup has a human API. At yours it's probably you. Half of knowledge workers say the only way to get an answer is to ask someone or book a meeting, a quarter of the week goes to searching, and the person being asked is interrupted every two minutes. Today at this venue we asked twelve people how many 'how do I' pings they answered last week; the median was [tally]." Sources on the slide: Atlassian 2025, Microsoft 2025.

**0:35–0:45, why now.** "Agents can now read your repo, ask a named human, and write the answer back under an approval you can audit. Multiplayer agent rooms are new, and we built one."

**0:45–2:15, the live demo.** QR on screen; judges join; a judge asks a deploy question and gets a cited answer; the presenter asks the gap question; `ask_human` routes to Person B; the approval card appears; a judge approves from their phone; the doc lands with the provenance footer; a late-joining judge asks the same question and gets it in two seconds. Closing line: "That question just got asked for the last time." State plainly that nothing was mocked.

**2:15–2:40, wedge and evidence.** "First wedge: engineering teams of five to fifty onboarding their next hire, sold to the engineering manager; the same loop extends to ops and field teams where the SOP is in someone's head. Every answer captured today is a doc nobody had to write." One demand data point from the venue tally or a judge's own reaction.

**2:40–3:00, velocity and ask.** "We built the room last month and AskOnce today, with the loop under test. Next ninety days: five design partners from this room, GitHub and Slack as knowledge sources, first hire a forward-deployed engineer. We'd like the interview." Stop.
